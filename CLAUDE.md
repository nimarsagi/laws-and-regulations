# Laws & Regulations — library for checking PRDs

EU AI Act (as amended in 2026), GDPR, and the Commission's guidelines on AI Act Article 5, split into one file per article, recital, annex or guideline section. Used to check PRDs made with chalky-prd.

## Where to go
| Task | Start at |
|---|---|
| Check a PRD against the laws | `review-map/CONTEXT.md` — it says what to read next |
| Look up an AI Act article or annex | `ai-act/CONTEXT.md` (list of every file, with sizes and what changed in 2026) |
| Look up a GDPR article or recital | `gdpr/CONTEXT.md` |
| What the Commission says about a banned practice | `guidelines-art5/CONTEXT.md` |
| What a defined term means | `_index/definitions.md` |
| When a rule starts to apply | `review-map/timeline.md` |
| Is a topic covered at all | `review-map/not-covered.md` |
| Which version of each law this is | `_sources/CONTEXT.md` |

Read the list file first, then only the files you need. Each law file has a header (citation, chapter, size, whether it changed in 2026) and ends with links to what it cites, what cites it, and the review areas that use it.

## Two kinds of files
- **Generated — never edit by hand:** `ai-act/`, `gdpr/`, `guidelines-art5/`, `_index/`. They carry a "generated by _system/build.py" line. To change them, change `_system/build.py` or a source in `_sources/`, then run `python3 _system/build.py`. It rebuilds everything, checks every source line landed exactly once, and checks every link.
- **Hand-written:** `review-map/`, this file, `_sources/CONTEXT.md`, `_system/build.py`. The originals in `_sources/` are never edited.

## Citing
`AI Act Art 26(1)` · `GDPR Art 6(1)(f)` · `GDPR Recital 71` · `Art 5 Guidelines para 243`. Quote the words that matter from the file, not from memory.

## Watch
- The AI Act was amended by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744. Many articles, dates and fines differ from the 2024 text. `▼M1` in a file marks the new wording; trust it over memory.
- The Art 5 guidelines predate that amendment and are not binding.
- The AI Act's recitals are not here; the GDPR is the 2016 text without later corrections.
